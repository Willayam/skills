# Source Map

This map is for navigation only. The skill references are paraphrased
operating notes derived from the sources below, not full-text conversions or
verbatim transcripts. For exact quotation, the user must verify against the
original source.

## Citation Convention

Each reference file cites with a bracketed short name, e.g. `[Stanford 2005]`,
`[Lost Interview]`, `[D5 2007]`, `[folklore.org]`, `[Isaacson]`,
`[Schlender]`. Multi-source attribution is fine: `[Isaacson], [Schlender]`.

When in doubt about whether a quote is verbatim or paraphrased, treat it as
paraphrased and tell the user to verify the original.

## Primary Sources (Public)

### Speeches and Addresses

- **Stanford Commencement Address, June 12, 2005** `[Stanford 2005]`
  - Three stories: connecting the dots, love and loss, death.
  - Full transcript: `https://news.stanford.edu/2005/06/12/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says/`
  - Video: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UF8uR6Z6KLc`
  - Canonical source for the mortality, meaning, calligraphy, firing-from-Apple,
    and "stay hungry, stay foolish" material.

### Long-Form Interviews

- **Smithsonian Institution Oral History, April 20, 1995** `[Smithsonian 1995]`
  - Conducted by Daniel Morrow for the Computerworld Honors Program.
  - Transcript: `https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/catalog/102714169`
    (Smithsonian transcripts also archived widely online).
  - Source for early-life formation, Atari, Wozniak, dropping out, and the
    "carpenter and the chest of drawers" framing of internal craft.

- **The "Lost Interview" with Robert X. Cringely, 1995** `[Lost Interview]`
  - Recorded for *Triumph of the Nerds*; outtakes released theatrically as
    *Steve Jobs: The Lost Interview* (2012).
  - Source for vertical integration, taste as exposure to the best work,
    process versus content, "real artists ship," and Jobs' diagnosis of
    Apple's mid-1990s decline.

- **Playboy Interview, February 1985** `[Playboy 1985]`
  - Conducted by David Sheff just before the Mac launch's aftermath and Jobs'
    departure.
  - Transcript: widely archived (e.g.,
    `https://reprints.longform.org/playboy-interview-steve-jobs`).
  - Source for early framing of computers as bicycles for the mind, the
    relationship between liberal arts and technology, and pre-firing voice.

- **Wired, "The Next Insanely Great Thing," February 1996** `[Wired 1996]`
  - Conducted by Gary Wolf during the NeXT era.
  - URL: `https://www.wired.com/1996/02/jobs-2/`
  - Source for design-is-how-it-works, taste as product judgment, and Jobs'
    skepticism toward then-current Apple leadership.

- **AllThingsD D5, May 30, 2007** `[D5 2007]`
  - Joint interview with Bill Gates; conducted by Walt Mossberg and Kara
    Swisher.
  - Video and partial transcripts at the AllThingsD/Recode archive.
  - Source for Jobs' mature reflections on Apple-Microsoft, integrated vs.
    open systems, and product strategy.

- **AllThingsD D8, June 1, 2010** `[D8 2010]`
  - Solo interview; conducted by Mossberg and Swisher.
  - Source for the iPad release context, integrated approach, "we're a
    software company," and Jobs' late-career lens on competitive dynamics.

### Television and Documentary

- **Triumph of the Nerds, PBS, 1996** `[Triumph]` / `[Nerds 1996]`
  - Three-part documentary by Robert X. Cringely.
  - Source for the "bozo explosion" articulation, the early-Apple narrative,
    and the rivalry-with-Microsoft framing.

- **The Pixar Story (2007), various** `[Pixar Story]`
  - Documentary covering Jobs' patron role at Pixar; pairs with Catmull's
    *Creativity, Inc.* for wilderness-years material.

### Keynotes and Product Launches

- **Macworld Boston, August 1997** `[Macworld 1997]` (Microsoft deal,
  return-to-Apple framing)
- **WWDC 1997 Fireside Chat** `[WWDC 1997]` (canonical "focus is saying no"
  source; archived on YouTube)
- **Macworld 1998** `[MacWorld 1998]` (back-in-the-black turnaround)
- **iMac Introduction, May 1998** `[iMac 1998]`
- **iPod Introduction, October 23, 2001** `[iPod 2001]`
- **Macworld 2007 (iPhone reveal), January 9, 2007** `[MacWorld 2007]`
- **iPad Introduction, January 27, 2010** `[iPad 2010]` (canonical "liberal
  arts intersect with technology" slide)
- **"Thoughts on Flash," April 2010** `[Thoughts on Flash 2010]`
  - Open letter from Jobs:
    `https://www.apple.com/hotnews/thoughts-on-flash/` (archived).

### Folklore and Team Memoir

- **folklore.org** `[folklore.org]`
  - Andy Hertzfeld's collected stories from the original Macintosh team,
    including "Reality Distortion Field," "Saving Lives" (the boot-time
    story), "Signing Party" (Mac case signatures), and "Pirates! Not the
    Navy."
  - URL: `https://www.folklore.org/`

### Eulogy

- **Mona Simpson, "A Sister's Eulogy for Steve Jobs," October 30, 2011**
  `[Simpson eulogy]`
  - Published in *The New York Times*:
    `https://www.nytimes.com/2011/10/30/opinion/mona-simpsons-eulogy-for-steve-jobs.html`
  - Source for "Oh wow. Oh wow. Oh wow." and the late-life family material.

## Secondary Sources (Cited but Paraphrased)

- **Walter Isaacson, *Steve Jobs* (2011)** `[Isaacson]`
  - The authorized biography. Page citations are not included in this
    skill's references — verify before quoting.

- **Brent Schlender and Rick Tetzeli, *Becoming Steve Jobs* (2015)**
  `[Schlender]`
  - Argues the wilderness years (NeXT and Pixar) matured Jobs into the
    leader who returned to Apple in 1997.

- **Carmine Gallo, *The Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs* (2009)** `[Gallo]`
  - Reverse-engineers the Jobs keynote method.

- **Ed Catmull, *Creativity, Inc.* (2014)** `[Catmull]`
  - Pixar co-founder's account; complements the Pixar arc of Jobs'
    wilderness years.

- **Steven Levy, *Insanely Great* (1994)** `[Levy]`
  - The original Mac story.

- **Alan Deutschman, *The Second Coming of Steve Jobs* (2000)**
  `[Deutschman]`
  - The NeXT/Pixar return narrative.

- **Tim Cook interviews and remarks** `[Cook]`
  - Various retrospectives on Jobs as manager and successor.

## Coverage Notes

- The Stanford 2005 transcript is the most reliably verbatim public source.
  Quotes drawn from there can be cited with high confidence.
- The Smithsonian 1995, Lost Interview, *Wired* 1996, and *Playboy* 1985 are
  long-form primary sources; quotation should still be verified against the
  archived transcript.
- Keynote material is paraphrased from public video. For verbatim use, watch
  the archived Apple Special Events video.
- folklore.org stories are firsthand recollections by Andy Hertzfeld and other
  team members — primary but selective.
- Isaacson, Schlender, Gallo, and Catmull are secondary works. Treat all
  attributed quotes as paraphrased unless the user supplies the page number.
