# Benjamin Hardy reference

Sources checked 2026-09-14. Quotes were verified against Goodreads, Strategic Coach publications, and podcast transcripts. Where a detail could not be confirmed, the text says so.

## Who he is

Benjamin Hardy is an organizational psychologist. He took a BS in psychology at BYU in 2013 and a PhD in industrial and organizational psychology at Clemson in 2018, with a dissertation on entrepreneurial courage and transformational leadership. His parents divorced when he was eleven and his father fell into addiction, which he cites as the origin of his interest in how people change. He served a two-year church mission in Pittsburgh. From 2015 to 2018, while still a graduate student earning about twelve thousand dollars a year, he was the most-read writer on Medium, with more than 100 million reads and an email list in the hundreds of thousands.

He and his wife Lauren became foster parents to three siblings from South Carolina in 2015, adopted them in February 2018, had twins in December 2018, and later a sixth child. They live in Orlando. His solo books are Willpower Doesn't Work in 2018, Personality Isn't Permanent in 2020, and Be Your Future Self Now in 2022. With Dan Sullivan, founder of Strategic Coach, he wrote Who Not How in 2020, The Gap and the Gain in 2021, and 10x Is Easier Than 2x in 2023. In 2025 he announced a next book, The Science of Scaling. Sullivan started coaching in 1974, was divorced and bankrupt on 15 August 1978 at thirty-four, and founded Strategic Coach with Babs Smith in 1988 in Toronto. He is now in his eighties and still teaching. Hardy writes and supplies the psychology. Sullivan supplies most of the tools.

## Core frameworks

### 10x is easier than 2x

Definition. A 2x goal keeps everything you do now and asks for more of it. A 10x goal cannot be reached by any current method, so it forces you to find the few things that scale and drop the rest. Sullivan says in the book, "A 2x goal would involve doing the same things you're doing now, only more of them. But a 10x goal jumps you out of that, beyond that." The book's own framing is that 2x is linear and finite, double effort for double reward, while 10x is nonlinear and returns more for less effort.

The core argument is that 10x is a statement about quality, not quantity. Hardy's line from the book is that 2x is trying to optimize the horse and buggy, and 10x is stepping back and inventing the car. A 10x goal is a filter. "Only when you make the goal big enough, 10x bigger, does it become absurdly and even comically obvious which strategies, relationships, or behaviors won't work." Under 2x everything looks important. Under 10x almost nothing qualifies. "With unrealistic, impossible, or 10x-level goals, the competition is lowest, the excitement is highest, and the pathway forward becomes simple and non-linear."

How to run it. First, name the 10x result specifically enough that you can say what would have to be true. Second, list everything you currently do and everyone you currently serve, and mark the 20 percent that could plausibly get you there. The test is whether it scales to the goal, not whether it is valuable. Third, treat the remaining 80 percent as the cost of the goal. Fourth, raise the standard on the 20 percent until it is ten times better than the market, not ten times more of it. MrBeast is the book's example. He told Hardy that a video 10 percent better does not get 10 percent more views, it gets around 400 percent more, and that "it's much easier getting 5 million views on one video than 100,000 views on 50 individual videos."

The past-10x-jumps exercise. Hardy has you list the 10x jumps you have already made. Learning to walk, learning to read, leaving home, your first job, becoming a parent, starting a business. Each one was a qualitative shift where you became a different person and dropped an old 80 percent without noticing. The point is that you already know how to do this.

### What you have to stop doing

Definition. The 80 percent is everything that produced your current result and cannot produce the next one. It is your existing clients, offers, habits, meetings, and identity. It feels safe because it works. Hardy said in 2025, "To 10X, you've got to let go of 80 percent of what you were doing before. You actually have to become a totally different person."

Why the 80 percent is defended. It pays the bills, it is where your competence lives, and people expect it. Dropping it looks like loss, and loss aversion beats the pull of gain. So the 80 percent survives every planning cycle by default. Nobody has to argue for it. Somebody has to argue against it.

How to decide what to kill. Three questions. Does this activity or customer move the 10x result, or only the current result? Would I start this today if I were not already doing it? Is this in my Unique Ability, or is it something I do because nobody else has been asked to? Anything that fails all three goes on the stop list. Hardy in Be Your Future Self Now, "Winners quit all the time. They just quit the right stuff at the right time."

### Who not how

Definition. When you hit a goal you cannot reach alone, the reflex is to ask "How do I do this?" That question makes you the bottleneck. The better question is "Who can do this?" Sullivan says, "How is a question that immediately puts a weight on your shoulders. Who is a question that immediately lifts it." The book itself is an example. Sullivan wanted the book written, Hardy was the who.

Why how-thinking caps growth. Every how you keep for yourself is a ceiling. Your output is limited to your own hours and skills, and your goals shrink to fit. "If you're committed to specific results, then at some point or other, you're going to have to face the truth that results are produced by Whos, not Hows." He also says, "If you have enough money to solve a problem, you don't have a problem," and that money is for buying solutions you do not want to do yourself.

The cost of doing it yourself is not the hours. It is the goals you never set because you know you cannot do the work alone.

Transformational relationships. The book splits relationships into transactional, where each side is watching what they get, and transformational, where both sides grow and the relationship changes who you are. "Relationships are how you transform as a person. Relationships are how you transcend your current limitations. Relationships are how you produce results." A who is not a contractor. A who is someone who owns the result and brings capabilities you do not have.

The Impact Filter. A Strategic Coach one-page tool. Six fields. Purpose, what you want to accomplish. Importance, the difference it will make. Ideal outcome, what done looks like. Best result if you act. Worst result if you do not. Success criteria, the specific completions that must happen. Sullivan calls it "the 30 minutes that saves you 30 days." You fill it in before you look for a who, because "you can't sell other people unless you're sold yourself," and because a who cannot own a result you have not defined.

### The gap and the gain

Definition. You can measure yourself against an ideal, which is the gap, or against where you started, which is the gain. "The way to measure your progress is backward against where you started, not against your ideal." Ideals move as you approach them, so measuring against them always produces a shortfall. "Each time you experience the GAP, it is the equivalent of a microtrauma to your system."

Why it kills momentum. Gap thinking makes every win feel like not enough, which drains the motivation the next step needs. A leader in the gap puts the whole team in the gap. "If you focus on what you lack, you lose what you have. If you focus on what you have, you gain what you lack."

How to run it. Measure backward on a fixed cadence. What has changed in the last quarter, the last year, the last ten years. Write three wins at the end of each day. Use ideals to set direction, never to score yourself.

### Future self

Definition. Hardy's central research claim. People are pulled by their view of the future more than pushed by their past. Psychologists call this prospection. "It's always your future that shapes who you are in the present. It's always you in the present that shapes and reshapes the meaning of your past." Your future self is a different person with different goals and standards, and the present decision should be made in that person's interest.

Identity precedes behaviour. Willpower Doesn't Work says, "Your behavior doesn't come from your personality. Rather, your personality is shaped by your behavior." Personality Isn't Permanent says, "Without a future self, you don't know who you are today. It doesn't matter who you are if you don't know who you plan to be." The 10x frame depends on this. You do not reach 10x by being the current person with more effort. You decide who the 10x person is and act from that identity now.

How to run it. Describe the future self in detail three years out. Then ask, for each present commitment, whether that person would keep it. "Decisions and actions are best when reverse-engineered from a desired outcome." His 2025 addition is to compress the timeline. Take the ten-year goal and ask what it would take in two or three years. "When you have a really high goal, most paths won't work."

### Environment over willpower

Definition. Willpower is a weak and depleting resource. Your environment, meaning the people, defaults, and constraints around you, decides most of what you do. "If we do not create and control our environment, our environment creates and controls us." So the work is to change the environment, not to try harder inside it.

Enriched environments. Put yourself where the standard is higher than yours. "Surround yourself with people who remind you more of your future than your past." "Don't join an easy crowd; you won't grow."

Forcing functions. A forcing function is a constraint you put in place that removes the option to do the wrong thing. Book a studio in advance and cancel the gigs. Go to the coffee shop without the charger. Pay in advance. Announce the date publicly. Hardy's own case is the foster children. Once three traumatized kids were in the house, the environment demanded a new person, and he became one. "100 percent is easier than 98 percent. It's easier to commit to something 100 percent."

How to run it. List the defaults that make failure easy. Change each one so the desired behaviour is the path of least resistance. Add one commitment that makes retreat expensive.

### Personality isn't permanent

Definition. Hardy argues against personality tests, against "that's just who I am," and against the idea that your past defines you. "Personality is not stable but changes regardless of whether you're purposeful about that change or not." "You are not a single and narrow 'type' of person." Identity is a story you tell about yourself, and stories can be rewritten. "A mistake repeated more than once is a decision."

How to run it. Name the trait you treat as fixed. Find one past instance where you acted otherwise. Decide what the future self would do and act that way once, then again. "Becoming psychologically flexible is key to personal transformation, not overattaching to your current identity or perspectives." For a company, the equivalent is "we are a company that does X." That is a personality claim, and it is as rewritable as any other.

### The four freedoms and Unique Ability as filters

Sullivan's four freedoms are time, money, relationship, and purpose. Freedom of time means you decide how your days are spent. Freedom of money means your income is not capped by your hours. Freedom of relationship means you work only with people you choose. Freedom of purpose means the work is aligned with what you care about. Each 10x jump should increase all four. If a growth plan reduces any one of them, it is a 2x plan wearing a 10x label.

Unique Ability is the Strategic Coach term for the activity where you have superior skill, energy, endless drive to improve, and results others notice. Sullivan says, "It's just a natural way that you take action to get results." It is the 20 percent for a person. Everything outside it is a who-not-how candidate.

The Entrepreneurial Time System backs this up. Focus Days are for Unique Ability work that produces revenue. Buffer Days are for preparation, delegation, and cleanup. Free Days are 24 hours with no work at all, no email, no thinking about the business. Sullivan takes 180 Free Days a year and asks clients to aim at 150 or more. Recovery is where the 10x ideas come from, and a calendar full of buffer work is the 80 percent made visible.

## Teaching stories

Dan Sullivan's own jumps. The book walks through Sullivan's life as a chain of 10x jumps. He began coaching in 1974 and took anyone who wanted help. On 15 August 1978, at thirty-four, he was divorced and bankrupt. He started journaling what he wanted, which he kept up for 25 years, and describes the shift from needing to wanting as the turning point. He narrowed to entrepreneurs only, then moved from one-on-one coaching to group workshops, then founded Strategic Coach with Babs Smith in 1988. Each jump meant dropping the clients and formats that had built the previous stage. The book's per-jump figures could not be confirmed from outside sources. Treat the structure as verified and the numbers as the book's claims.

Michelangelo and the David. The book opens with the David, carved from a block of marble other sculptors had rejected as flawed. Michelangelo's method, as the book tells it, was to see the figure already inside the stone and remove everything that was not David. This is the book's image for 10x. The result is not built by adding. It is revealed by subtraction.

MrBeast. Jimmy Donaldson told Hardy that a video 10 percent better gets around 400 percent more views, and that one great video beats fifty decent ones. The point is that quality returns are nonlinear, and that Donaldson never let the 80 percent, meaning volume for its own sake, into his process.

Chad Willardson. At 24, in a financial adviser training program, Willardson decided to work only with clients investing at least 100,000 dollars, against his manager's advice. He went six months with no clients. His first client invested 600,000. By the end of the 18-month program he had raised the minimum to 250,000 and had 30 million under management. Setting the floor high looked like losing clients. It was choosing the 20 percent.

Carson Holmquist. Holmquist founded Stream Logistics, a construction transport company, in 2012 at 26 and grew it from 3 to 30 people by 2017. Routine freight was 95 percent of clients and a race to the bottom on price. High-stakes freight was 5 percent of clients and 15 percent of profit. He stopped taking routine work entirely. With the same 40 employees, revenue went from 22 to 36 million dollars in under three years.

Linda McKissack. A real estate agent who, after coaching, changed her model to only meeting clients and hired an assistant to do everything else. Her income went up around 10x.

Hardy's foster children. In 2015 Hardy and Lauren took in three siblings, ages roughly three to eight, from a county in the bottom decile for economic mobility. The children had lived in a trailer with drug-addicted parents, been given cough syrup to sleep, and missed fifty consecutive school days. One boy would lose the ability to move his limbs during anxiety attacks. Hardy rebuilt their environment, with Montessori school, sports, consistent routines, twelve hours of sleep, and one-on-one academic help. Within a few years the diagnoses had eased and the children were adopted in February 2018. Hardy uses this both for environment over willpower and for forcing functions.

## His voice

Direct. Backed by named research, with Dweck, Seligman, and prospection studies cited. Warm toward the person, hard on the excuse. He repeats the frame rather than softening it. Short declarative sentences, then a longer one to explain. He asks what you are going to stop.

## Questions he would ask about an organisation

Hardy wrote for entrepreneurs, but the frame translates. A company of a few thousand people is a person with a very large 80 percent and a very well-defended one. The questions.

1. What is the 10x result, stated so that someone could check it? Not "grow" or "transform." Ten times what, by when, for whom. If the room cannot say it, there is no filter and every project survives.
2. What is the company's Unique Ability? Not the mission statement. The specific thing where it has superior skill, the market notices, and it keeps getting better. A strong platform and a strong brand are assets, not abilities. What does the company do with them that nobody else can?
3. Which 20 percent of the business could plausibly produce 10x? Which products, which customers, which teams. Name them. Then ask what share of headcount, roadmap, and executive attention they get today. The gap between "could produce 10x" and "gets the attention" is the whole diagnosis.
4. What is the 80 percent, and who defends it? In a large company the 80 percent is the roadmap, the metrics reviews, the features shipped because a competitor shipped them, the teams that exist because they once mattered. Each has an owner whose career depends on it. Ask who loses if it is cut, and whether the leader will have that conversation.
5. What would "stop doing" mean here? A list of products, segments, meetings, and metrics that end, with dates. Not a reorg. If the list is empty, the plan is 2x.
6. Is the company measuring in the gap or the gain? Optimization cultures live in the gap by design. Every dashboard shows distance to target. What has the company gained in ten years that it no longer counts?
7. Who is the future company? Describe the version three years out that has 10x'd. What does it do every day that the current one does not? What does it refuse that the current one accepts?
8. Where does the company still ask how instead of who? Which capabilities does it insist on building in-house out of habit? Which results could a who own outright?
9. What forcing function would make retreat expensive? A public commitment, a killed product, a separate unit with its own P&L and no access to the old 80 percent.
10. Would the plan increase the four freedoms for the people doing the work, or just add more? A 10x plan that costs the best people their time and purpose is a 2x plan with worse morale.

## How he would think about AI and agentic coding

Sullivan has already said it in plain words. "Today we have the added advantage of automation. So who can be a machine. It can be an AI program." Strategic Coach's current teaching is that the whos who take care of everything outside your Unique Ability now include software, and that AI multiplies individuality rather than flattening it. The line from their recent podcasts is that in a world of AI imitation, creative people will get wildly more creative to stand apart from the machines.

Hardy would take it further, because the 10x math changes in two ways.

First, AI is a who for the 80 percent. Most of what a company protects is work that had to be done by people because there was no alternative. Reports, tests, integrations, maintenance, first drafts. When an agent can own a result end to end, that result stops being a how the company must staff. The 80 percent becomes cheaper to drop, because the argument "we need those people for that work" weakens. Letting go gets easier, and the excuse goes away.

Second, AI collapses the cost of how for the 20 percent. The reason a 10x goal used to be filtered so hard is that most paths were unaffordable. If a strong team can now try ten times as many paths, the filter changes. The limit stops being "what can we build" and becomes "what do we want, and what would be worth ten times more to the customer." That is a question about taste, standards, and Unique Ability, not capacity. This is the shift from needing to wanting at company scale. Needing asks what we can afford. Wanting asks what we would build if building were nearly free.

The trap is obvious to him. A company that uses AI to do its current 80 percent faster is doing 2x with better tools. Optimizing the horse and buggy at 10x speed. The 10x version uses the productivity gain to fund a separate bet on the 20 percent, protected from the old roadmap, staffed by people operating in their Unique Ability with agents as the whos underneath them. The question is never "how much more can we ship." It is "what would we ship if the cost of shipping were no longer the constraint, and what would we stop."

He would also point out that agentic coding changes who counts as a who for an outsider. One builder with agents can now own a result that used to need a team. That makes an outside builder a plausible who for a large company's 20 percent, not a vendor for its 80 percent.
