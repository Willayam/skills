# The user's taste and standing constraints

Collected from about sixty design sessions between August and October 2026. These are defaults. A product's own brand and the user's latest words override them. Paste the relevant lines into builder briefs.

## Usually liked

- Restraint with care in the details, "as if Apple made them". Clean, calm and minimal, but never bland. "Too bland" and "timid" got rejected as often as "too much".
- Minimal or rounded styles. A modern sans serif by default. Serif only when a direction calls for it.
- Soft edges over sharp corners, squircles. Hard borders over large soft shadows.
- Monochrome or one accent. Colour carries meaning, such as state or identity, rather than decoration.
- Real product feel: frosted glass and premium dark surfaces where the product already uses them, a solid charcoal primary action, one clear primary button per view.
- Things that stay put. Fixed sizes, tabular numbers, no layout shift, cards of equal height in a row.
- Direct manipulation, with controls placed where they act.
- Compact density that still breathes. Every element earns its place: "every single thing on the screen needs to fight for its life."
- Familiar conventions over invention: "how is this usually handled?", "not reinvent the wheel". Reuse the product's existing components.
- The smallest change to the product that does the job: "what would be the minimum design surface change you could do to achieve the same thing?" A feature that fits in an existing row beats a new screen, step or setting.
- Messages to the product's customers give the least detail that lets them act. "just say that a url is unavailable, don't need to give too much detail to clients." For a taken name, say it is unavailable, not why or who has it.
- Playfulness and easter eggs only where the product is playful, such as a party app or a personal site.

## Usually disliked

- Clutter, repeated information, "boxes in boxes", wasted space, long explanatory copy, counts everywhere.
- Glow, gradients, pulsing and "make it sell" decoration.
- Jumpy, random or aggressive motion. Delayed tooltips. Hovers that change several things at once. Shapes that grow and shrink.
- Destructive actions shown as plain text links. Controls covering the content, such as a speaker's face.
- Misalignment, uneven heights, clipped text, fake or substitute fonts, low-resolution screenshots.
- Components shown shrunk inside mock screens. "these are too small." Show a component at true size, with its real surroundings cropped close.
- Generic shadcn defaults when the product deserves its own feel. In a product that already ships the shadcn look, though, the user has asked to keep it.

## Standing constraints

- Phones are a first-class target. Check 390 px every round.
- Light and dark when the product has both.
- Plain copy in the product's language, sentence case, no em dashes.
- Performance matters. A heavy or laggy effect loses to a simpler one.
- Only options that can actually ship: licensable fonts and assets, data the backend can provide.
- The user reviews from a phone or another Mac as often as from this one.

## How the user gives feedback

- By ID or name, often mixing parts: "the graphics from C5 and the background from C8".
- By screenshot of a fragment, with or without a note.
- By dictation, sometimes in Swedish, with transcription errors. Read for intent and say how you read it.
- By pasting the comment bar's text, or a URL with slider values in its query string.
- By one letter for a quick pick. Final picks arrive in chat, such as "v22 is the one i want".
