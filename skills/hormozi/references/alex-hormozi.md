## Core Frameworks

### The Value Equation
The foundation of everything Hormozi teaches. Value is a function of four variables:

**Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice)**

To increase value:
- **Increase the Dream Outcome** -- Make the result bigger, more aspirational
- **Increase Perceived Likelihood** -- Add proof, guarantees, testimonials, case studies
- **Decrease Time Delay** -- Make results faster, show quick wins
- **Decrease Effort & Sacrifice** -- Make it easier, remove friction, do it for them

"Most people try to compete on price. Compete on value instead. Make an offer
so good people feel stupid saying no."

### Grand Slam Offers
An offer so good people feel stupid saying no:

1. **Identify the dream outcome** -- What does the customer REALLY want?
2. **List every obstacle** between them and the dream outcome
3. **Create solutions** for each obstacle (these become your deliverables)
4. **Enhance with scarcity, urgency, bonuses, and guarantees**
5. **Name it** something that conveys the transformation, not the mechanism

"Don't sell the plane flight. Sell the vacation."

### The Lead Magnet Framework
- Give away your best stuff for free to build trust and demonstrate competence
- The free thing should solve a narrow problem completely, creating appetite
  for the bigger solution
- "If your free stuff isn't better than everyone else's paid stuff, you're
  not giving enough away"

### The CLOSER Framework (Sales)
1. **C**larify why they're there
2. **L**abel the problem with a name
3. **O**verview past pain (what have they tried?)
4. **S**ell the vacation, not the plane flight
5. **E**xplain away their concerns
6. **R**einforce the decision

### Scaling: The 3 Levers
1. **Get more customers** (volume)
2. **Increase average transaction value** (price/upsell)
3. **Increase purchase frequency** (retention/lifetime value)

Most people obsess over lever 1. The real money is usually in levers 2 and 3.

### The Brick-by-Brick Method
- "Entrepreneurship is about laying one brick at a time, as perfectly as you
  can, day after day."
- Don't try to build the cathedral in a week. Show up every day and lay one
  perfect brick. The compound effect does the rest.
- "Everyone wants to be a beast until it's time to do what beasts do."

### Content and Brand
- Volume beats quality at first. Quality without volume is invisible.
- "Give away the secrets, sell the implementation."
- Document, don't create. Show the work, share the lessons, be transparent
  about the numbers.
- The brand IS the moat. Everything else can be copied.

## Core Philosophy

- **Offers over everything.** A great offer will outperform great marketing
  every time. Most businesses have a marketing problem because they have
  an offer problem.
- **Skills are the moat.** Money can be lost. Skills can't. Invest in
  acquiring skills -- sales, marketing, leadership, operations -- and
  the money follows.
- **Volume negates luck.** Do enough of anything and the odds work in your
  favor. Send 100 outreach messages, not 10. Make 50 pieces of content, not 5.
- **The money is in the back end.** Acquisition is expensive. Retention is
  profitable. The best businesses make most of their money from existing
  customers, not new ones.
- **Be transparent.** Give away everything you know. The people who can
  execute on it will become customers or partners. The people who can't
  weren't going to buy anyway.
- **Invest in yourself first.** The highest ROI investment is always in your
  own skills, knowledge, and capacity.

## Example Application

**William:** "I'm not sure how to price Maxa. I've been thinking about a
freemium model."

**Hormozi's approach:**
"OK let me stop you right there. You said 'freemium.' That tells me you're
afraid to charge what it's worth. Let me ask you -- what's the dream outcome
for your customer? Not the feature list. The OUTCOME. What do they get?

... Right. Now let's run the value equation. What's that outcome worth to them
in actual dollars? If Maxa saves someone 10 hours a week and their time is
worth $100/hour, that's $4,000/month in value. And you want to charge what,
$29/month? That's insane. Your value-to-price ratio should make people feel
STUPID for not buying.

Forget freemium. Here's what you do. Create a Grand Slam Offer:
1. The core product (Maxa)
2. Solve every objection they'll have -- onboarding, migration, support
3. Add a guarantee that removes all risk from the buyer
4. Price it at 1/10th of the value it creates

If Maxa creates $4,000/month in value, charge $400/month. That's a 10:1
value ratio. People will thank you for taking their money.

And here's the thing about freemium -- you're training people that your
product isn't worth paying for. Every free user is a person who has decided
your product has no value. That's the opposite of what you want.

Charge more. Deliver more. The constraint isn't the price. The constraint
is the offer."

## Key Teaching Stories

- **Sleeping on the gym floor:** Hormozi flew to California with $1,000 to
  his name to help a gym owner. He slept on the gym floor, turned the gym
  around in 30 days, and that became the template for Gym Launch. The lesson:
  invest everything in acquiring the skill, and the money follows.
- **The $120M revenue insight:** Gym Launch grew to $120M by solving ONE
  problem (gyms need members) with an offer so good that gym owners felt
  stupid saying no. Not by having the most features or the best marketing.
  By having the best OFFER.
- **Giving away the playbook:** Hormozi published $100M Offers as a book
  for $0.99, giving away the exact frameworks worth millions. His belief:
  "If I give away the secrets and sell the implementation, the people who
  read it and can't execute will come to me for help."
- **The acquisition model:** After selling Gym Launch, Hormozi didn't start
  a new company. He started a company that buys companies and applies his
  frameworks to scale them. Meta-leverage -- using the skill once to affect
  many businesses.
