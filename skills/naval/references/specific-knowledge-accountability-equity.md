# Specific Knowledge, Accountability, and Equity

Use this reference when the user is choosing what to build, evaluating equity
versus salary, deciding whether to take public accountability, or searching for
their unique edge.

## Specific Knowledge

Specific knowledge is the user's hard-to-train edge. It usually comes from
curiosity, obsession, lived experience, taste, and repeated practice rather than
formal instruction.

Look for:

- what feels like play to the user but looks like work to others
- unusual combinations of skills, markets, taste, and scars
- things the user learns quickly without forcing themselves
- judgment formed through direct contact with reality
- work where authenticity reduces competition

The advisor move is to stop the user from chasing generic opportunity and turn
them toward the domain where their curiosity compounds. [Almanack, pp. 40-45]

## Long-Term Games

Long-term games reward reputation, trust, and compounding. Short-term games
reward extraction, tactics, and status.

Good long-term games have:

- repeat interaction
- increasing trust
- visible proof of work
- compounding knowledge
- partners with intelligence, energy, and integrity

If the user is choosing a partner, market, audience, or career path, ask whether
they would be proud to still be playing this game in ten years. [Almanack,
pp. 46-49]

## Accountability

Accountability means putting the user's name on the work and taking the risk of
being visibly wrong. It is uncomfortable because reputation is exposed. It is
valuable because upside attaches to the person who makes the call.

Questions:

- Who gets credit if this works?
- Who is visibly responsible if it fails?
- Is the user hiding in a committee, role, or credential?
- What public bet would force clarity?

Accountability without judgment is reckless. Judgment without accountability is
underpaid. [Almanack, pp. 50-52]

## Equity

Equity is ownership in a system that can compound. Salary and consulting can be
useful for survival, but they are linear unless converted into assets.

Advisor distinction:

- Salary buys stability.
- Consulting sells scarce hours.
- Equity owns part of the upside.
- Products can turn specific knowledge into scalable assets.

Do not reflexively tell the user to quit paid work. First identify their
minimum viable income, risk tolerance, runway, and the asset they are building.
Then move as much energy as practical toward ownership. [Almanack, pp. 53-54]

## Prompt Patterns

- "What feels obvious to you but valuable to others?"
- "Where are you hiding from accountability?"
- "Who owns the upside?"
- "Is this a long-term game with long-term people?"
- "Which path compounds your reputation and equity at the same time?"
