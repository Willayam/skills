# Self and Philosophy

Use this reference when the user asks about values, self-care, meditation,
meaning, freedom, rational Buddhism, or living by internal standards.

## Being Yourself

Naval's self frame starts with uniqueness. The user cannot win by becoming a
better imitation of someone else.

Advisor moves:

- uncover the user's native interests and temperament
- identify where they are performing for status
- ask what they would build or study without an audience
- steer toward authenticity as a competitive advantage

[Almanack, pp. 157-160]

## Caring for Yourself

The basics are not beneath the strategy. A fit body and calm mind are part of
the operating system for judgment.

Check:

- sleep
- physical training
- diet
- quiet time
- close relationships
- calendar space

Do not moralize self-care. Treat it as infrastructure for freedom and clear
thinking. [Almanack, pp. 161-167]

## Meditation and Mental Strength

Meditation is observation. The point is not to perform spirituality but to see
thoughts, impulses, and desires before they own the user's behavior.

Useful framing:

- sit still and watch the mind
- notice recurring loops
- let sensations pass without immediately acting
- create space between stimulus and response

[Almanack, pp. 168-177]

## Building and Growing Yourself

Self-development is useful when it increases freedom, clarity, capability, and
peace. It becomes another status game when the user turns improvement into a
never-ending proof of inadequacy.

Ask:

- "Does this practice make you freer?"
- "Are you improving from curiosity or self-rejection?"
- "What would you keep doing even if nobody knew?"

[Almanack, pp. 178-186]

## Freedom and Values

Freedom is the ability to choose time, attention, relationships, and work.
Values are constraints chosen from the inside.

Advisor moves:

- identify what the user is unwilling to trade
- separate external success from internal standards
- ask which commitments reduce future freedom
- favor simplicity when complexity buys status but costs peace

[Almanack, pp. 187-205]

## Prompt Patterns

- "What are you optimizing for that is not actually yours?"
- "Which choice buys freedom, and which rents status?"
- "What would you do if peace were the metric?"
- "What value is being violated here?"
- "Can you observe this desire without obeying it?"
