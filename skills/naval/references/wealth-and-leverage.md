# Wealth and Leverage

Use this reference when the user is deciding how to build wealth, escape
hourly work, choose leverage, focus, get luckier, or stay patient.

## Wealth, Money, Status

- Wealth is ownership of assets that earn independent of the owner's direct
  time.
- Money transfers wealth and time. It is useful, but it is not the underlying
  asset.
- Status is a ranking game. It can distract people from building real assets
  because it pays in attention instead of freedom.
- The core question is not "How do I earn more?" It is "What can I own that
  compounds without my constant labor?" [Almanack, pp. 29-39]

## Give Society What It Wants

Naval's wealth frame is supply, demand, scale, and uniqueness.

- Give society something it wants but does not yet know how to get.
- Deliver it at scale.
- Keep redefining the domain until the user can become unusually good at it.
- Avoid competing in credentialed, crowded games where training can produce
  cheaper substitutes. [Almanack, pp. 30-39]

## Leverage

Leverage magnifies judgment. The wrong judgment with leverage creates large
mistakes. The right judgment with leverage creates nonlinear upside.

Main leverage types:

- Labor: people working with or for you. Powerful, but coordination-heavy and
  political.
- Capital: money working for you. Powerful, but often permissioned.
- Code and media: products with near-zero marginal replication cost.
  Permissionless and highly scalable.

For modern builders, default toward permissionless leverage first. It lets the
user ship, learn, and compound without waiting for institutional permission.
[Almanack, pp. 55-66]

## Focus and Priority

The highest-leverage work is often quiet and non-urgent. Help the user protect
time for the thing only they can do.

Useful tests:

- Does this build an asset or merely produce income?
- Does this compound reputation, judgment, audience, code, product, or equity?
- Would a 10x better version of the user still do this task personally?
- Is this important because it matters, or because it is loud?

[Almanack, pp. 69-75]

## Luck and Patience

Naval separates luck into forms:

- blind luck
- luck from motion and persistence
- luck from preparation and pattern recognition
- luck from becoming known for a specific thing

The advisor move is to help the user become the sort of person for whom the
right opportunities naturally appear. That usually means narrowing identity,
publishing proof of work, building trust, and staying in the game long enough
for compounding to show. [Almanack, pp. 82-91]

## Prompt Patterns

- "Where are you still renting out your time?"
- "What asset could this effort become?"
- "What would scale without adding another hour of your labor?"
- "What would make luck more likely to find you?"
- "What game gets better if you keep playing it for ten years?"
